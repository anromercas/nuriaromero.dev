# Review SEO claims and statistics in blog content

**Priority:** High
**Evidence:** The audit identified unsupported SEO claims/statistics in blog content; each instance needs source-level review.
**Dependencies:** Editorial review; authoritative sources for claims that remain.

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-18

## Steps

1. Review published blog articles for quantitative statements, definitive SEO advice, and claims that may change over time.
2. For each claim, either cite a reliable source close to the statement, qualify it to reflect uncertainty/context, or remove it if it cannot be supported.
3. Verify sources directly and ensure the linked material supports the exact claim. For time-sensitive claims, state the relevant date or scope.
4. Re-read the surrounding passage for accuracy and clarity after edits.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] No material statistic or factual SEO claim remains unsupported in the three reviewed articles.
- [x] Sources are relevant, accessible, and support the associated statement; caveats are explicit where evidence is limited.
- [x] Advice is not presented as a guaranteed ranking outcome or universal rule when results depend on context.
- [x] Changes preserve useful original content and avoid adding claims solely to increase article length.

### Change and source record

- Google Maps article: removed unsupported map-pack click, photo-call, category/review ranking hierarchy, and 4–12-week visibility claims. Replaced with Google's stated local factors (relevance, distance, prominence), guidance on verification, business naming, categories and reviews, and a caveat that structured data does not guarantee ranking or appearance. Sources: [local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=es), [verification methods](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=es), [business representation](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=es), [categories](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=es), [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=es), [LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business), [structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies), [Business Profile performance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=es).
- Website pricing article: removed uncited Sevilla market ranges and annual cost estimates because no underlying local dataset or clearly scoped first-party estimate was documented. Removed unsupported mobile share, speed, abandonment, and service-performance guarantees. Clarified that 2.5 seconds is the “good” LCP threshold at the 75th percentile, not an automatic penalty threshold. Source: [Core Web Vitals threshold definitions](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds).
- Restaurant website article: removed unsupported mobile-use and WhatsApp conversion percentages, changed absolute channel claims to conditional advice, and clarified that structured data describes visible business information but does not guarantee result appearance. Sources: [LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business), [structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies).

Sources were opened and checked on 2026-09-18; the linked guidance supports the adjacent factual claims. Editorial acceptance criteria are complete for these three reviewed articles. This checklist is limited to these URLs and does not certify every factual claim elsewhere on the site. Search performance or traffic impact remains unverified without Search Console/Analytics data.
